Try it before you look

  1. Predict: how many $1 million blocks make $1 billion? How many $1 billion blocks make $1 trillion?
  2. Compare $1 million, $1 billion, and $1 trillion. Watch the scale bar as the camera pulls back.
  3. Switch to “Stack to space.” Would a trillion $1 bills reach the Moon? The Sun? Then try the debt.

One simple rule. Enormous consequences.

Choose $1 or $100 bills. For the same dollar amount, $100 bills need 100× fewer notes and 100× less volume. The $1 amount is unavailable in $100 bills. A bill is modeled as 0.10922 mm thick. A thousand bills form a stack about 10.9 cm tall.

Everyday objects compares packed money with familiar objects at one shared scale. Reference sizes are approximate examples, not universal dimensions. Money blocks rearranges bills into compact rectangular stacks. Stack to space puts every bill in one vertical column, beginning at Earth’s surface.

Zoom changes the camera, never the amount of money.

Model, sources & GitHub instructions

Dimensions: 6.14 × 2.61 inches × 0.0043 inches = 0.155956 × 0.066294 × 0.00010922 m. Thickness is an approximate classroom assumption; real notes vary with wear and compression. No air gaps or packaging are included. Blocks preserve total bill volume. Internal lines are grouped boundaries or illustrative layers, not necessarily individual bills.

Scale: block geometry and the 1.7 m person share a physical scale. The space view uses a linear height scale at every zoom level; stack width and celestial icons are enlarged for visibility. A logarithmic zoom control changes the linear camera scale. A column this tall could not physically support itself; this is a dimensional comparison, not a feasible construction. Gross federal debt is an accounting liability, not a pile of existing currency.

Empire State Building: 443.2 m (1,454 ft) to the antenna tip; the stepped facade and footprint are simplified for comparison. Height source: Council on Vertical Urbanism / The Skyscraper Center.

Space references: 100 km conventional edge of space; ISS reference altitude 400 km (varies); Moon’s mean center distance 384,400 km, less Earth’s mean radius 6,371 km, gives 378,029 km above the surface to the Moon’s center in this schematic alignment. Sun’s reference distance 149,597,870.7 km (1 AU), less 6,371 km. Directions are schematic; the Sun and Moon are not always aligned.

Sources: USAFacts / Treasury debt data, August 2026: $40.2 trillion; Treasury: Debt to the Penny; U.S. currency dimensions (Congressional Record); NASA Moon facts; NASA Earth facts. Debt snapshot checked September 27, 2026; edit the input when a newer figure is available.

Upload: this is a self-contained HTML file with embedded graphics and code. Upload it to a GitHub repository as index.html, then enable GitHub Pages for the branch containing it. It also opens directly in a browser, works offline, and needs no external libraries, images, or API keys.
